Debit Cards at Pickup: Acceptance, Holds and Refusals

The question behind the refusal

Has anyone dealt with this specific "Anti-Revolut" discrimination?

The question comes from an r/Revolut account of a Thrifty LAX refusal. The poster describes broker prepayment, presenting a physical card and being refused because of its issuer. That is an unverified individual account. A commenter suggests prepaid classification as an explanation; the suggestion is not confirmation of the card’s classification.

What the published rules actually support

Thrifty’s General Policies, sections F and G distinguish credit and debit authorization holds. The U.S. debit section allows up to $200.00 for credit cards or $500 for debit cards in addition to estimated rental charges. These are holds, not a rental price or refusal penalty. The page exempts qualifying established Blue-Chip members with a Visa card already on their profile from the incremental hold.

The same page requires a physical card, excludes digital-wallet cards and requires a major credit card for its PayNow rentals. Location requirements control. It does not state a Revolut-specific blanket ban or identify the LAX poster’s card. Broker prepayment alone does not establish that a reservation was Thrifty PayNow. These published requirements do not establish whether the reported refusal complied with that particular reservation.

The retrieved Revolut U.S. prepaid cardholder agreement names Lead Bank as issuer and identifies the covered products as prepaid. Its effective date is November 18, 2025. That identifies this agreement’s program, not every Revolut card worldwide or the poster’s card.

MoneyWeek’s report by Sam Walker, published August 6, 2026 concerns the transition of UK customers to bank accounts. It does not establish acceptance at a U.S. rental counter. Banking status and a particular rental’s card requirement answer different questions.

A separate UK Financial Ombudsman decision, DRN-4949399 concerns a Revolut-funded rental dispute with an unnamed merchant. The decision did not require further action by Revolut. Its reasoning considered the booking’s credit-card condition and contractual no-show definition. This is a decision about that complaint, not a U.S. rental policy or a ruling on the LAX account.

Our comparison: do not let one record answer another question

This is our analysis of the evidence above, not an additional rental policy. A rejection can name the issuer while the written condition concerns a card product. Keep both statements. Replacing the recorded explanation with a commenter’s more plausible explanation would erase the very discrepancy that needs checking.

Start with a comparison sheet rather than a verdict about Revolut. Put the reservation terms beside the refusal record, then identify the exact condition each addresses. If the paperwork says credit card and the refusal says issuer, record that mismatch without deciding which explanation must be true. If the card’s own agreement identifies a prepaid product, attach that agreement rather than inferring classification from the logo.

Read the later success as a changed transaction

The thread’s later edit describes getting a car from Hertz at a downtown hotel and using Apple Pay. Compared with the initial Thrifty LAX account, the supplier, location and presentation method changed. That is not a controlled comparison. It cannot isolate the issuer as the reason for either outcome.

A successful second transaction is evidence about the second transaction. It is not a corrected first transaction. In particular, an account of wallet acceptance elsewhere cannot override a physical-card condition in the original supplier’s published terms. Nor does collecting a replacement car establish that the first prepayment was refunded.

Keep the refusal and the refund as separate outcomes

Build a short event record: reservation accepted, card presented, refusal explained, booking status recorded, refund response received. Leave any undocumented stage blank. A later rental belongs on a separate line with its own supplier and location, rather than at the end as proof that everything was fixed.

For the disputed booking, ask the broker and supplier to identify the condition they relied on and to explain how it relates to the recorded refusal. Preserve their separate responses. A counter conversation and a later written case response may differ; that difference is a comparison to make explicit, not a gap to fill with an assumption.

The useful outcome of this exercise is a narrower question: does the reason recorded for this booking match its disclosed pickup condition and the actual card product? That question can be supported with documents. A broad claim that every Revolut card must be accepted cannot be established from this source set.
