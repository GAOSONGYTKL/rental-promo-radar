A Student Car Rental Discount Starts With the Right Offer

What SIXT puts in writing

SIXT's official coupons page says:

Students get a discount when booking their prepaid car rental online.

Its student booking button leads to a student-verification service. That establishes a route to check, without supplying a universal discount percentage or guaranteeing acceptance for a particular rental.

The provider's young-driver guide adds:

Generally, the Young Driver Surcharge cannot be waived for car rental under 25.

Student status alone does not establish a fee waiver. Any exception must come from the terms of the agreement being used.

A rental-company heading does not identify the owner of every promotion displayed underneath it. In the Student Beans result, the eye-catching number and the rental-company name come from different parts of the page. Hotels.com owns the listed hotel saving, so our rental comparison removes that figure from Enterprise's column.

The UNiDAYS page creates a different attribution problem: a loyalty-program invitation appears alongside an Expedia hotel promotion. We record the invitation as the Enterprise item and leave the hotel percentage with Expedia. Reading both pages together gives no verified Enterprise student percentage to recommend from either of these displays. That finding is narrower than saying Enterprise never offers a student benefit; it concerns the evidence on these particular pages.

The Avis example needs a different correction, because the rental brand is correct but the retrieved student page does not substantiate the percentage displayed in its search preview. Our table therefore withholds that number as a usable student offer instead of converting a search preview into booking terms. Enjoy Travel passes the brand-attribution check as a broker's own advertised offer, while its affiliated-university condition still limits who can rely on the claim.

The campus discussion and AI summary fail another check: their percentages describe reported or aggregated claims, rather than verified eligibility for the reader's rental. The reviewed University of Miami and Northwestern pages add a separate reason to be careful: their student permissions depend on the rental's purpose and local program rules. A directory mention, a membership invitation and a rental discount consequently receive different labels in this audit, even when a search engine places them together. The useful result is a shorter list of claims that actually concern the chosen rental, with unrelated hotel promotions removed before booking. This review evaluates attribution and supporting text; checkout savings and a cheapest-provider comparison remain untested.

One university booking, one reported outcome

One renter described being both a student and a part-time university employee, booking through the university's negotiated-rate SAP channel:

But when I went to the counter, where I handed in a passport AND my driver's license, they didn't say anything, they just gave me the keys, charged me the corporate rate with no fee, and let me go.

This is an individual account, not a promise to other students. A university agreement may provide an exception, but the account does not establish which contractual clause produced the outcome. Do not select a business-only rate for a personal trip without permission under that agreement.

What still needs money at pickup

For deposits, SIXT's official Salt Lake City page states:

The amount depends on the location and vehicle.

There is no single student deposit amount established by that statement. Obtain the requirement for the pickup location and vehicle before committing to a prepaid booking.

Treat the discount, any age surcharge and the deposit as separate questions. The reviewed wording does not establish a universal student exemption from either of the latter requirements.

Choose the route whose issuer and eligibility you can identify. Leave any unsupported percentage out of the decision.
