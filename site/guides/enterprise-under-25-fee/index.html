Enterprise Under 25 Fee: Match the Amount to the Rental

Enterprise describes its under-25 fee as approximately $25 per day on average, but publishes separate New York and Michigan amounts by age and rental type. The average is not a nationwide fixed charge. Match your rental to the car or Truck Rental section before using a state fee, and verify any claimed exemption in the terms of your particular program.

Different headings are not contradictory prices

Our comparison uses three identifiers together: pickup state, age band and rental business. Remove any one and a correct number can become the wrong number for the trip. This is a source-matching exercise, not a test of a completed reservation.

For a 23-year-old in Michigan, the car section supplies $15.50 per day, while the Truck Rental section supplies $24.00. The calculated difference is $24.00 minus $15.50 = $8.50 per day. That is the distance between two published entries, not an extra charge to add to either one. Do not combine them, average them or assume the higher amount supersedes the lower one.

For an 18-year-old in New York, the two entries are $68.75 and $121.50 per day. Their calculated difference is $121.50 minus $68.75 = $52.75 per day. A comparison that drops the business heading conceals a substantial distinction. It does not show which service a particular reservation uses, and the difference is not a saving available merely by changing a label.

Both calculations use only the FAQ amounts in the table, read October 20, 2026. They exclude base rental charges, taxes, protection products and every other charge. No tax-inclusive total or booking price is implied.

There is a further classification trap. The FAQ's car-rental vehicle list includes pickup trucks for ages 21-24, while a separate heading provides Truck Rental fees. The presence of the word truck in a vehicle description therefore does not settle which fee schedule governs it. Our recommendation is to establish the booking business and applicable terms, rather than assigning a Truck Rental surcharge to every pickup truck.

If a quote differs from a published entry, record the actual young-renter line and ask which policy applies. Do not silently replace it with the average. Equally, a broad requirements page without a dollar amount is not evidence that the more detailed fee page has been cancelled. A genuine disagreement requires competing statements about the same rental scope; these two labelled schedules describe different scopes.

What the age bands change financially

Within the car section, Michigan's published 18-20 amount exceeds its 21-24 amount by a calculated $18.50 per day: $34.00 minus $15.50. In New York the calculated difference is $35.50 per day: $68.75 minus $33.25. Inputs again come from the official FAQ read October 20, 2026.

These are comparisons between age bands, not instructions for splitting a rental at a birthday. The reviewed FAQ does not establish whether a driver turning 21 or 25 during the rental automatically changes the charge. It also does not establish young-renter-fee proration for a partial day or a universal cap. Those items remain unconfirmed here.

Our practical distinction is between a published daily unit and the rules that determine how many such units are charged. Knowing the former does not prove the latter. Do not divide a daily fee into an hourly amount, count calendar dates as billable days, or promise that returning early removes a unit without the applicable rental terms.

For the approximately $25 average, even the daily input is not yet a confirmed location-specific amount. Multiplying it by the trip length would produce an illustration based on an average, not a personal quote. We have therefore kept that average outside the fixed-amount CSV rows and have not presented an assumed trip total as money the renter will owe.

A discounted rate is not a verified fee waiver

The reviewed Enterprise pages do not establish an automatic young-renter-fee exemption for every military traveler, government employee, loyalty member or student. They also do not establish current Enterprise-specific USAA or AAA waiver amounts. Those waiver amounts and conditions remain unconfirmed in this review; benefits offered by another rental brand cannot fill the gap.

Our benefit check asks what charge the promise names. A lower base rate addresses the vehicle rate. Permission for a colleague to drive addresses additional drivers. Neither statement, by itself, names the young-renter fee. Treating them as interchangeable can turn a real benefit into a saving its terms never offered.

The official-versus-leisure distinction matters for the same reason. An employee's status does not make a holiday official business. The current official-travel FAQ's warning for ages 18-20 also prevents us from turning the age exception into a universal fee exemption. We do not assign a zero charge or a partial waiver to either group without applicable terms specifying it.

If an employer, school or other eligible program provides a genuine rental agreement, examine its age-surcharge clause rather than just its headline discount. Establish the covered driver, travel purpose, booking method and whether the fee is removed entirely, reduced, or still payable. Where the agreement does not say, leave the waived amount unconfirmed. Do not borrow an unrelated group's credentials to manufacture eligibility.

For a reward-day booking, apply the same reading discipline: base-rate coverage is not coverage of every line on a bill. Our conclusion is limited to the official guide's base-rate wording; we have not priced a redemption or tested a checkout. Keep the young-renter charge unresolved until the applicable reservation terms address it.

Resolve the fee that belongs to this rental

Use the published schedule to identify the relevant entry, then obtain the charge attached to the intended booking through Enterprise's reservation entry. If relying on an exemption, retain the clause naming the young-renter fee alongside that booking. An average, a different business's schedule and an unnamed discount are three different kinds of evidence; none substitutes for the applicable fee or waiver term.
